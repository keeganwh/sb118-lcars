SKIN

Three ways the same screen could look

Layout and behaviour are settled — this is only about the furniture. Each treatment picks one visual language and holds to it, instead of mixing a filled badge, outlined circles, a boxed toggle and outlined pills on one screen. Same sim text in all three so only the chrome is being judged.

A · One bar

Header and sim title merge into a single dark bar; the accent survives as one slim block on its leading edge. Nothing else is filled or outlined — the tools are just text and icons. Two bands of furniture instead of three, and about 40px more sim on screen.

B · LCARS spine

The accent leaves the header and runs down the writing edge as one continuous elbow — which is what LCARS actually is. The sims tab is a segment of that run rather than a sticker on top of it, and the dimmed segment below is the same spine continuing. The most on-theme of the three, and the most colour.

C · Quiet

Nothing is filled except what is currently on. Hairlines separate the bands, the accent appears once as the edge stripe, and the sims handle is that stripe swelling into a chevron. The furniture recedes until the sim is the only thing on screen with any weight — the calmest for long writing, the least obviously LCARS.

A · what the tab should say

A on its own, with four ways of naming what the chevron opens. All keep A's rule: one filled accent shape on the screen, nothing else outlined.

1 · Chevron over the word, set vertically

Costs no writing width. The word is rotated, so it takes a beat to read the first time — but only the first time.

SIMS

All three keep what the lab settled: toolbar at the top, grouped as Copy / B / I / Insert / Format / Tools, a hide control in the top corner, and a sims handle on the right edge that opens the drawer. They are not exclusive — the bar from A, the spine from B and the quiet toolbar from C can be mixed. Try each skin: B leans hardest on the accent, so it changes most between Prime and High Contrast.
